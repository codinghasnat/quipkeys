We killed sloppy typos.

Your phone solved this years ago. Its keyboard knows where your thumb landed and guesses the word you were going for, so fat-finger taps come out right. The keyboard on your desk never got that trick. It types exactly what you hit, and a spell checker underlines it afterwards. QuipKeys brings the phone trick to physical keyboards.

How it knows what you meant

⌨

Where your fingers are

Hitting the key next door is a common slip. Hitting one across the board isn't. Every guess is scored by distance on a real keyboard.

Your rhythm

Two keys pressed almost together are probably swapped. A hesitation before a key often means it went wrong.

The whole sentence

A language model built from 1.4 billion words of Wikipedia reads the sentence and can repair a word you typed earlier, even a misplaced space.

Your words

Hit Backspace straight after a fix and it's undone for good. Names, slang and jargon stay exactly how you type them.

Spell it how it sounds

Switch on Spell by sound and QuipKeys also reads words spelt by ear, then lets the sentence pick between sound-alikes. It's built with dyslexic typists in mind, and it's free like everything else here. It's off by default, so ordinary typing works exactly as before.

Stays on your computer

  • No internet, everThe dictionary and language model are built in. No account, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Forgets as you goIt only looks at your last few words, and forgets them on Enter, a click or an app switch.
  • Knows when to back offIt stays out of password fields, terminals and code editors, and you can pause it with one shortcut.
